Definition

The 8 Facets of a Full-Stack Data Scientist

A modern Full-Stack Data Scientist is an end-to-end technical practitioner capable of taking an ambiguous business question, formulating the mathematical loss function, engineering the distributed data layer, building frontier architectures, and managing production inference with clear monitoring and feedback loops.

1. Theoretical Depth

High-dimensional geometry, linear transformations, multivariable backpropagation calculus, and convex optimization duality.

2. Software Craftsmanship

Type-hinted modular OOP, defensive design patterns, CLRS algorithm design, asymptotic complexity, and automated unit testing.

3. Systems Engineering

Database query planners, buffer pool management, B+ Trees, ACID transactions, and distributed consensus via Raft.

4. Modeling & Causality

Statistical learning, regularized regression, gradient-boosted trees, and counterfactual reasoning via Causal Forests.

5. Modern Deep Learning

Transformer architectures, Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE), SwiGLU, FlashAttention, and pretraining LLMs from scratch.

6. Production MLOps

Hardware-aware quantization (INT8/FP4/AWQ), high-throughput serving (vLLM/Triton), and hybrid dense vector GraphRAG.

7. Governance & Safety

Model alignment (DPO/RLHF), differential privacy budgeting, prompt injection defense, and EU AI Act compliance.

8. Frontier Specialization

Domain mastery in either Quantitative Finance (HFT / volatility regimes) or AI for Science (DeepMind AlphaGenome / AlphaFold).

◇ The Industry Engineering Gap — What Academia Omits & How to Master It

MIT OCW and Stanford Online teach rigorous theory, but no university lecture course covers day-to-day production tooling. Master this production stack in parallel:

  • Environment & Linux CLI: Bash scripting, process management, POSIX tooling (awk, sed, grep), and SSH.
  • Version Control: Git trunk-based workflows, interactive rebases, cherry-picking, and pull request reviews.
  • Containerization & Orchestration: Multi-stage Dockerfiles, Docker Compose, Kubernetes manifests, and Helm charts.
  • API Engineering: High-performance async microservices with FastAPI, strict Pydantic v2 schemas, and gRPC.
  • CI/CD Automation: Automated linting (Ruff), type checking (MyPy), testing (PyTest), and container deployment via GitHub Actions.

Pacing

Recommended Execution Schedules

Select an execution schedule tailored to your existing background, technical strengths, and weekly time commitment.

Track A: Comprehensive Self-Paced

18–24 Months · 12–18 hrs/week

Structure: Allocate 2 to 3 months per pillar. Work through all theoretical problem sets and complete the hands-on capstone before advancing.

Target Audience: Beginners or professionals transitioning from non-technical fields who require complete mathematical and engineering depth.

Track B: Accelerated for Software Engineers

9–12 Months · 20–25 hrs/week

Structure: Fast-forward through Pillar III (SWE) and Pillar IV (Databases/Systems). Allocate 80% of effort to Pillar I (Math), Pillar II (Stats), Pillar V (ML), Pillar VI (CS336 LLMs), and Pillar VII (MLOps).

Target Audience: Backend, cloud, and distributed systems engineers who already write modular code and deploy containerized services.

Track C: Accelerated for Quantitative / STEM Grads

9–12 Months · 20–25 hrs/week

Structure: Fast-forward through Pillar I (Linear Algebra & Calculus) and Pillar II (Probability & Stats). Focus heavily on Pillar III (Software Construction & 6.006), Pillar IV (Distributed Systems), The Industry Gap, Pillar VI (CS336 LLMs), and Pillar VII (MLOps).

Target Audience: Physicists, mathematicians, and quantitative researchers who need software craftsmanship and production systems expertise.

Zero-Cost Access Strategy

All resources in this curriculum are completely free and un-gated. Follow these exact methods to access materials without paywalls:

1. MIT OpenCourseWare (OCW)

Always 100% free and open. All lecture notes, syllabi, problem sets, and exams can be downloaded directly in PDF without creating an account. Full video playlists are available on the course pages or directly via MIT's official YouTube channels (@mitocw and @MIT6S191).

2. Stanford Online & edX Free Audit Route

When clicking through to edX courses (such as Statistical Learning or Databases), always select the "Free / Audit Track". This grants full, unrestricted access to all lecture videos, readings, and course materials without paying for a verified certificate.

3. Stanford Departmental Public Portals

Stanford CS and EE departments host public, un-gated websites for their premier courses (e.g., cs229.stanford.edu, cs224n.stanford.edu, cs231n.stanford.edu, cs336.stanford.edu, stanford-cs329s.github.io, stanford-cs324.github.io). These hubs publish slides, reading lists, lecture notes, and assignment specifications openly.

4. YouTube Repositories

Both Stanford Online (@stanfordonline) and MIT OCW (@mitocw) maintain official, high-definition lecture playlists for every flagship lecture series featured in this roadmap.
